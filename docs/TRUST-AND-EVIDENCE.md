# Trust and Evidence

Expert-led businesses often mix research, professional experience, client cases, teaching metaphors, and personal meaning. Each can be valuable. They should not be presented as if they all carry the same type of support.

## Claim protocol

For any important claim, answer:

1. What exactly is being claimed?
2. For whom, where, and under which conditions?
3. What is the evidence class?
4. What source or case supports it?
5. What credible disagreement or limitation exists?
6. When was it last checked?
7. Who is accountable for approving its use?

## Scientific work

When a topic is scientific:

- prefer current primary sources and authoritative reviews;
- state whether a paper was directly reviewed;
- separate correlation, mechanism, and causation;
- record sample and context limitations;
- include credible counter-evidence;
- have a qualified human review consequential interpretations.

“Scientific process” should describe a reproducible way of asking, testing, and updating—not be used as a badge for certainty.

## Lived and practitioner knowledge

Repeated practice can produce real expertise before formal research exists. Label it `practitioner-derived`, document the cases and boundaries, and invite tests. Do not downgrade it to “mere opinion,” and do not upgrade it to universal proof.

## Personal and interpretive practices

Symbolic, reflective, intuitive, or meaning-making practices may be presented as interpretations or metaphors when that is what they are. Avoid medical, diagnostic, or deterministic claims unless properly qualified and supported.

## Case evidence

Record:

- starting situation;
- intervention;
- observable result;
- alternative explanations;
- consent for use;
- what must remain private;
- whether the case is representative or unusual.

## Corrections

Corrections are system improvements. Log:

- original claim;
- reason for correction;
- changed evidence status;
- artifacts affected;
- human approval;
- date.
