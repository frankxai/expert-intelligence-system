# Ideal Audience

## Primary — the established expert

They have a real body of work: courses, frameworks, clients, students, research, a book, a community, a substantial audience, or several years of recorded practice.

They are not AI beginners in the usual sense. They may already use ChatGPT or Claude. Their unmet need is architecture:

- their best knowledge is scattered;
- AI output sounds plausible but not distinctly theirs;
- evidence, citations, and boundaries are inconsistent;
- their team cannot reliably use their judgment;
- valuable insight is trapped in calls and personal memory;
- tools are multiplying without becoming a coherent operating system.

### Buying trigger

A new course, book, cohort, team, platform initiative, or AI transition exposes the cost of keeping the method tacit.

### Desired progress

Make the expert's system teachable, traceable, portable, and useful across research, creation, delivery, and business decisions.

### Trust requirements

- no “clone” language;
- no loss of ownership;
- private and public material remain separate;
- real evidence and limitations are visible;
- the system works with existing tools;
- the expert's judgment remains final.

## Secondary — the experienced emerging founder

They have credible experience and a recurring problem they can help solve, but no mature corpus, audience evidence, or product architecture yet.

Their risk is building a large course or “personal brand” before discovering a narrow, useful transformation. The first system should help them run customer discovery, name their method, gather evidence, and deliver a small pilot.

## Institutional — the expert platform

An education company, expert community, accelerator, or membership organization can use the method to help multiple experts become AI-native while keeping each expert's corpus and voice separate.

The institutional promise is not one shared bot. It is a repeatable architecture for many sovereign expert systems.

## Not a fit

- people seeking instant authority without domain work;
- growth programs that require fabricated scarcity or guarantees;
- organizations that intend to ingest expert IP without clear rights;
- anyone expecting autonomous medical, legal, financial, or other consequential advice;
- users who want content volume but will not review claims or source material.
